
# Social Engineering

## Definition
Social engineering is a technique attackers use to manipulate people into sharing sensitive information or performing unsafe actions. Instead of attacking technology directly, attackers take advantage of human behavior.

## Key points
- Phishing uses fake emails or messages to trick victims into clicking malicious links or sharing information.
- Spear phishing targets a specific person or organization with personalized messages.
- Whaling targets important people, such as executives.
- Vishing uses voice calls to trick victims.
- Smishing uses SMS text messages to trick victims.
- Pretexting happens when an attacker creates a fake story or identity to gain trust.
- Baiting offers something attractive to encourage a victim to take an unsafe action.

## Example
An employee receives an email that looks like it came from the company's IT department. The email asks the employee to click a link and enter their password. This is a phishing attack.

## Exam tip
Remember the differences: phishing uses deceptive messages, vishing uses voice calls, and smishing uses SMS messages. Spear phishing targets specific victims.
